# URJIVA: Product Boundary & Research Rules

## 1. Core Operating Principle (Non-Negotiable)
The URJIVA decision engine shall **never recommend any energy transition pathway that reduces production reliability, process temperature capability, or throughput** relative to the facility's current baseline. 

Every recommendation generated by the engine must be, at minimum, production-neutral. Furthermore, every proposed pathway must demonstrably improve the operator's financial standing, operational risk position, or both. Industrial facilities operate on guaranteed uptime; any transition pathway introducing unacceptable process risk is invalid by definition.

## 2. Product Scope and Boundaries
To maintain analytical rigor and actionable outputs, the product boundaries are strictly delineated.

### 2.1 Current Product Scope
- **Target Architecture:** Single-factory decision support for integrated process heat and power.
- **Geography:** Industrial facilities operating within India.
- **Focus:** Optimization of commercially mature technologies that can be deployed within standard capital cycles without requiring fundamental redesigns of the core manufacturing process.

### 2.2 Future Research Tracks (Out of Current Scope)
- **Multi-Site Optimization:** Fleet-level energy arbitrage and multi-facility grid balancing.
- **Large Continuous-Process Plants:** Heavy industry (e.g., primary steelmaking, cement clinker production) requiring fundamental chemical or thermal process substitutions.
- **Thermal Power Plants:** (See Section 6).

## 3. Decision Criteria Hierarchy
The engine's optimization algorithms and constraint solvers must evaluate all potential pathways according to the following strict hierarchy. A pathway failing a higher-order criterion must be discarded prior to subsequent evaluation.

1. **Thermodynamic & Process Feasibility:** Does the technology physically meet the precise heat (temperature, pressure, enthalpy) and electrical load-profile requirements of the specific process?
2. **Reliability & Operational Risk:** Does the pathway guarantee existing or improved uptime levels? Does it mitigate supply chain risks (e.g., fuel availability) without introducing unproven maintenance requirements?
3. **Economics Under Uncertainty:** Is the Levelized Cost of Energy/Heat (LCOE/LCOH), payback period, and NPV viable under stochastic modeling of fuel pricing, grid tariffs, and capital costs?
4. **Emissions & Policy Eligibility:** Does the pathway meaningfully reduce Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions? Does it maximize eligibility for carbon markets, BEE PAT scheme benefits, and other state/central incentives?

## 4. Knowledge Source Mandates
The integrity of the decision engine relies entirely on the veracity of its underlying parameters. **No invented, assumed, or unverified efficiencies, costs, or emission factors are permitted.** 

# Data Sources & Governance Rules

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Binding for all knowledge-base entries and decision-engine calculations  
**Last updated:** September 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the only permissible sources of quantitative and qualitative parameters used by the URJIVA decision engine, and the mandatory rules that govern how those parameters are recorded, versioned, validated, and communicated to users.

The goal is to ensure that every recommendation remains:
- Traceable to authoritative sources
- Transparent about uncertainty
- Conservative when data quality is insufficient
- Production-neutral (never reduces reliability, process temperature capability, or throughput relative to the current baseline)

---

## 2. Allowable Data Sources

All parameters (efficiencies, costs, emission factors, temperature limits, resource availability, policy rules, etc.) must originate from one or more of the following categories only:

### 2.1 Government & Regulatory Entities
- Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)
- Central Electricity Authority (CEA)
- Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)
- NITI Aayog
- Equivalent state-level energy or industry departments

### 2.2 Intergovernmental & Authoritative Scientific Bodies
- Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)
- International Energy Agency (IEA)
- Peer-reviewed reports commissioned or endorsed by the above organisations

### 2.3 Peer-reviewed Academic Literature
- Engineering, thermodynamic, and energy-economics journals that carry a clear publication date and DOI (or equivalent permanent identifier)

### 2.4 Verifiable OEM Technical Data
- Official technical data sheets and performance guarantees published by equipment manufacturers
- Must include test conditions, rating basis, and publication or revision date

### 2.5 Verified Public Institutional Datasets
- NREL, World Bank, and other institutional datasets that carry clear provenance and version identifiers

**No other sources are permitted.** Marketing claims, blog posts, secondary summaries without primary citation, or internal estimates without documented basis are excluded.

---

## 3. Mandatory Governance Rules

### 3.1 Provenance & Versioning
Every parameter stored in the knowledge base must carry the following metadata:
- Source organisation
- Document title, report number, or dataset name
- Publication or access date
- Confidence tag: **High** | **Medium** | **Low**

When multiple sources exist for the same parameter:
- The highest-confidence source, or the most recent government / intergovernmental source, takes precedence
- Any material conflict is recorded in the knowledge-base entry

### 3.2 No-Invention Rule
The decision engine must never fabricate efficiencies, costs, emission factors, resource availability, or any other quantitative value.  
If required data is missing or falls below acceptable confidence, the engine must either:
- Refuse to issue a firm recommendation, or
- Clearly flag the limitation and widen the reported uncertainty range

### 3.3 Uncertainty Communication
- Economic outputs (payback, ROI, savings, net present value) are always reported as ranges under defined sensitivity cases (minimum default: fuel-price variation of ±20 %).
- Reliability and data-quality scores are visible to the user.
- A single-point estimate is never presented as definitive when underlying data carries material uncertainty.

### 3.4 Progressive / Living Knowledge Base
The system is designed as a living decision layer, not a static model.
- All data is versioned.
- Tariff, fuel-price, technology-performance, and policy modules are structured so they can be refreshed from official sources without rewriting the core engine.
- The current implementation uses the best available static snapshots. The architecture already supports periodic (and later automated) updates as new official data is published.

### 3.5 Validation Against Reality
Before any recommendation pathway is treated as production-ready:
- Baseline energy, cost, and emission calculations are cross-checked against published BEE / CEA / sector benchmark ranges for the same industry and process temperature band.
- Material deviations from established ranges trigger a data-quality warning that is surfaced to the user.

### 3.6 User-Protection Principle
The system is intentionally conservative.  
When data confidence is low, process constraints are poorly specified, or required inputs are incomplete, the engine prefers to withhold a strong recommendation rather than risk guiding the user toward a pathway that could waste capital, time, or production reliability.

---

## 4. Implementation Notes

- Every new knowledge-base entry must pass a provenance check before being merged.
- Confidence tags and source metadata are mandatory fields in the knowledge-base schema.
- Frontend and report outputs must surface uncertainty ranges and data-quality notes; these are not optional display elements.
- Periodic review cadence (recommended minimum: quarterly) against new BEE, CEA, MNRE, and IEA publications is part of normal maintenance, even while the team remains student-led.

---

## 5. Change Control

Any proposed relaxation of the source categories or governance rules above requires explicit team decision and documented justification. Silent expansion of allowable sources is not permitted.

## 6. Data Ingestion Protocol
To maintain a traceable, auditable, and easily updated knowledge base, all new technology parameters, efficiency metrics, and cost data entered into the system must conform to a strict schema.

Every quantitative data point must be logged with:
- **Source:** Direct citation, DOI, URL, or specific document reference.
- **Date:** The publication, vintage, or verification date of the data.
- **Confidence Tag:** A structured metric indicating data maturity (e.g., commercial deployment vs. pilot phase) and source reliability.

## 7. Restriction on Thermal Power Plants
**Utility-scale thermal power plants are strictly designated as a research track for the next 12–18 months and remain outside the current product boundary.**

*Justification:* Direct coal replacement and retrofitting of utility-scale thermal power plants involve grid-level dispatch dependencies, complex regulatory lock-ins (e.g., long-term PPAs), and massive scale requirements that differ fundamentally from single-factory captive power and heat dynamics. Attempting to model these within the current engine would dilute the accuracy, focus, and immediate commercial viability of the core industrial product.
