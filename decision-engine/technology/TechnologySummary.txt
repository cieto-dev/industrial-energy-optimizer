Excellent. Now we're moving into the **Engineering Layer (G1)** of your Industrial Energy Transition Optimizer.

From the SIH architecture, this folder belongs to the **Technology Models** layer, whose responsibility is to represent each technology (biomass, solar, electrification, storage, waste heat, etc.), evaluate its technical capability, efficiency, temperature limits, and other engineering characteristics before passing feasible options to later stages.

# Before understanding the files, understand the business problem

Imagine you own a textile factory.

Your factory currently uses:

* Coal Boiler
* Diesel Generator

You ask your software:

> "What technologies can replace coal?"

The software **cannot** simply answer:

> Solar
> Biomass
> Hydrogen
> Heat Pump

Because every factory is different.

Instead, it must answer:

* Is Biomass available?
* Is Solar possible?
* Can Heat Pumps reach the required temperature?
* Is Hydrogen realistic?
* Does the factory have enough roof area?
* Is the electrical grid strong enough?

Only after answering these questions can the software recommend technologies.

This entire responsibility belongs to the **Technology Folder**.

# Why is the project designed this way?

Many beginners expect one file like:

```text
technology.py
```

containing everything.

That quickly becomes difficult to maintain because it mixes:

* Technology discovery
* Eligibility rules
* Engineering calculations
* Performance estimation

Instead, the project separates responsibilities:

```text
technology_matcher.py
        │
        ▼
Finds relevant technologies

technology_filter.py
        │
        ▼
Removes unrealistic candidates

technology_engine.py
        │
        ▼
Calculates engineering performance
```

This follows the **Single Responsibility Principle**, making each module easier to understand, test, and extend. For example, adding a new technology (such as green hydrogen) mainly affects the matcher and engine, while the optimizer and reporting modules remain unchanged.

---

# Common beginner mistakes

1. **Confusing matching with optimization**

   * Matcher answers *"Can this technology be considered?"*
   * Optimizer answers *"Which technology is best?"*

2. **Putting financial calculations into the engine**

   * The engine should estimate engineering quantities (capacity, efficiency, fuel).
   * Detailed financial refinement belongs to the finance layer.

3. **Skipping filtering**

   * Evaluating every technology wastes computation and creates unrealistic scenarios.

4. **Mixing business rules with physics**

   * Engineering equations and business constraints should remain separate.

# Suggested Improvements

As the project grows, you could enhance this folder by:

* Maintaining a versioned technology database.
* Supporting technology plug-ins so new technologies can be added without changing the engine.
* Including confidence levels for engineering assumptions.
* Tracking evidence and source references for each technology parameter.
* Caching repeated engineering calculations for similar factories.

| Receives Data From                                      | Sends Data To              |

**One-sentence takeaway:** The **Technology Folder** is the engineering brain of the system—it decides **which technologies are technically appropriate, removes impractical ones, and computes the engineering performance data that every downstream module (scenarios, optimization, finance, and reporting) depends on.**
